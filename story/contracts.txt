
Beat 1 — The camp in the woods
Assignment

ALEKSEJ: You are the one they talk about in the square. Good. I was beginning to think I would have to do this myself, and I am too old and too fat for the woods.

There is a camp in the field above the wagoners camp. The one leading them is called Ondra. He took a carter's load on Thursday alongside his cock, which was unnecessary. The town council is willing to pay 200 groschen for justice, I am willing to add 100 on behalf of every man.

[Henry: "Why not send the guard?"]

ALEKSEJ: Because the guard would march in broad daylight, in full uniform, with a bard singing of their exploits, and find cold fires and even colder latrines. You on the other hand know how to deal with such tasks.

Report

ALEKSEJ: Ondra as well? Good. He was the only one of them worth worrying about.

Take the silver, take it — do not count it in front of me, it is insulting. (laughs) You know, in the east we had a man who counted his pay in front of his lord and his council every week for thirty years. Every single week. And then one week he reveived coins, still hot from the forge. Didn't count anything after that.

Beat 2 — The mine
Assignment

ALEKSEJ: Now this one is not thieves beside a road. This is thieves on a hill.

Silver has been seemingly falling through a hole in a carts bed. Not much at a time — that is the clever part, a little and a little and nobody's tally is wrong enough to shout about. They are dug in atop a hill, right behind one of the mines. A man called Vávra keeps the tally for them. He was a lumberjack before he became a thief, so he will not run, he will go down swinging something sharp and heavy.

[Henry: "Who wants them gone?"]

ALEKSEJ: Everybody. The miners, their foreman, the mines owner, the town mint and me. 

Report

(Henry hands over the transfer schedule. Aleksej reads it. There is a beat — a fraction too long — before he answers.)

ALEKSEJ: ...Well. I … did not expect those bandits to keep such a consistant tally of their, uhm, earnings.

It tells of another gaggle of bandits transferring the silver... You have made this my problem now, Henry. I hope you understand that. A man who knows where the silver goes and does nothing about it is Vogelfrei.

Beat 3 — The convoy
Assignment

ALEKSEJ: They will pass near Miskovitz I think, their leader, a hedge knight, is rumoured to have a shy sweetheart there. You best ambush them before they get to the village.

Don't underestimate the knight however, he is rumoured to have anything but the sweet disposition of his damsel. Heard, that when he is part of an army, he is assigned green boys with pitchforks and sent to soften up the enemy. He enjoys it.

[Henry: "And the silver?"]

ALEKSEJ: Bring back what you can carry. What you cannot carry, burn it. Oh right, it's silver (laughs), so you'll have to just break your back then.

Report

ALEKSEJ: Oh my, that is a very large haul. Keep all of it, you deserve it.

(after a pause) We had a captain in the north, far north, who paid us in salt for two winters, because there was no coin between the Dvina and God and he had raided a salt merchant, having overheard two mean in a tavern talking about him and having misheard silver instead of salt. Can you imaging that?

STAGING NOTE: He is overpaying. He knows it. This is the last generous moment he can afford and it is buying goodwill from the man who just destroyed his silver.

Beat 4 — The second camp
Assignment

ALEKSEJ: Rabble have burned down a mill and decided to make camp on the ashes. Can you imaging that? Sleeping on a bed of smouldering coals? must be quite warm.

A man called Kuneš has them in hand, or thinks he does. Go and be quick, I want to eat before dark, they say eating after dark makes you sleep bad.

Report — the unguarded moment

[Henry: "The one leading them wasn't called Kuneš. And he was wearing Sigismund's colours."]

ALEKSEJ: ...Say that again.

What did he wear. Exactly. Not what you think he wore.

[Henry describes it.]

ALEKSEJ: (the warmth comes back — too fast, too complete) Hah. Well. That explains a great deal, actually.

There is a captain of Sigismund's working this country. I have heard the name twice this month and dismissed it twice, and that was stupid of me. He is buying men — deserters, brigands, whatever will hold a pitchfork — and he is paying them out of somebody's silver. (taps the table) Somebody's silver, Henry.

It was never thieves on a hill. It was their payroll.

STAGING NOTE: The one line of the mod where the mask is off. Brief and cold — no anger, no fluster, a man going very still and asking a precise question. Everything after it is invention, delivered fast, because he is building it as he speaks.

He named the wrong man. Kuneš was supposed to be leading that camp. Somebody outranked him, wearing stores that were not due to come out for months. That is what Aleksej has gone still about, and the player has no way to know it yet.

Beat 5 — The patrol
Assignment

ALEKSEJ: I have found his men. Or they have found us, I am not certain which way it goes.

They are riding the low road past the fields tomorrow. (and here, for the first time) I do not know their road past that and I do not know their strength. That is the trouble. It could be nothing and it could an army, and I would not send you if there were anyone else to send.

[Henry: "You're sending me anyway."]

ALEKSEJ: I am. (beat) Go carefully, my friend. I have grown used to your competence.

Report — the slip

(Henry comes back. He should not have.)

ALEKSEJ: ...Boje.

(genuinely surprised, then covering it) You, you defeated them? and walked back here on your own two legs. (laughs) I have rarely seen a warrior of your might, my friend.

ALEKSEJ: Sit. Sit. I am going to tell you something I should have told you at the very beginning of our aquaintance, and you may be angry with me for it.

There is no captain. There is a man of god. A Hungarian — Kanizsai, archbishop of Gran, or he was. He dropped a crown on the wrong bald spot last summer and Sigismund took his castle, income and then his dignity. Only didn't fuck his wife, being a churchman, he had none.

With the silver he had left he came here and decided to build an army, parts of which you have been successfully turning into red stains on our fields.

[Henry: "Why tell me now?"]

ALEKSEJ: Because you keep coming back alive, and a man who keeps coming back alive eventually finds things out for himself. I would rather he found them out from a friend.

STAGING NOTE: Every word of this is true. He is handing over his own paymaster because a broke archbishop in Hungary is worth nothing to him and makes an excellent thing for Henry to chase. He omits one clause: he is the man collecting.

Beat 6–8 — Silence

No lines. He is gone from Kuttenberg, absent from Raborsch, and the only voice in the mod stops for three beats. That silence is doing more work than any dialogue we could afford — do not fill it with a note, a letter read aloud, or a parting line nailed to his door.

Beat 9 — The marsh, aleksejs last stand.

(Only part that is not certain, if a dialog before the assault is possible, otherwise we'll put this in a letter explaining it all)

ALEKSEJ: Stop there. Not because of my men — they will not move unless I tell them. Stop there because I want to say this once and I will not shout it,

This water smells of home. Did you know that? Same reeds, same rot, same flat grey sky sitting on it like a lid. I have been in this country nine years and I found Zaslawye in a bog outside Kuttenberg. (a short laugh) God is not subtle.

[Henry: "Who were you?"]

ALEKSEJ: I served a man. I have not said his name aloud since I crossed the Vistula, and I am going to say it now, because after today there is nobody left who could be hurt by it.

Švitrigaila.

(beat) You have never heard of him. That is the joke. I gave him everything I had and you have never heard of him.

[Henry: "And your men?"]

ALEKSEJ: They came with me from Zaslawye. Not for the priest — they have never heard of the priest either and believe in a different version of god either way. They came because I said we would go somewhere with better land, and I was right, the land here is very good.

They cannot go home. Neither can I. So they will die in a marsh for an archbishop who stopped writing to me in the autumn, and they will not complain about it, because they never do.

(drawing) Do not make us wait, Henry. It is unkind.
